Strategy case study

Cyberette — from unsystematized pipeline to a repeatable engine

Senior Strategy Consultant engagement owning North American go-to-market for a deepfake and synthetic-media forensics platform. The product had one live customer and no repeatable sales process — pipeline lived in scattered notes, qualification broke down on the first hard question, and the CRM was out of date. I rebuilt the positioning, picked the markets, and designed the process that would make revenue repeatable.

Positioning

Detect → Analyse → Prosecute. Competitors return a probability score — useless once money moves or a case reaches court. I repositioned the product around court-admissible forensic evidence with a documented chain of custody: the same output serves a law firm's exhibit, a talent agency's takedown, and a police case file. One wedge, three markets.

Market selection & rationale

Reputation & brand-protection agencies

Urgent brand risk, discretionary budget, one agency carries many clients — fast and repeatable.

Legal (media law, privacy, national security)

Clear court use case with per-matter budget — a longer chase, but the demand is real.

IAM & identity channel partners

Embeds into KYC flows the partner already owns — lowest friction, fastest path to volume.

Deliberately sequenced: three low-friction plays fund the ramp and generate references, while slower law-enforcement, government, and banking cycles mature underneath. First revenue was never gated on the slowest buyer.

Qualification, redesigned

"How many fraud cases do you have?" got no answer — the work was manual and uncounted. I replaced it with a process-cost qualification: hours spent per case today, cases dropped for lack of proof, exposure when evidence fails. Every deal mapped to a named champion, economic buyer, and decision maker before it could reach proposal.

The sales engine

Each stage carries an exit test — no deal advances without a named role identified and the next step booked. Weekly pipeline hygiene (stage, next step, aged deals flagged) is enforced by CRM automation, not memory.

Result

5 enterprise accounts closed at six-figure deal values, 40+ strategic partnerships established, and a regional product-market fit reached by turning an ad hoc pipeline into a process every rep could run the same way.
